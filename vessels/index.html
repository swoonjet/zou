    Not yet resonant

    Strike plays the base note and the note the pot's ratio makes. A pot that is slightly out of tune beats audibly.

    Extrapolation 1 · the instrument

    A shelf that is a scale

    Eight vessels, each built from a different pair of polygons, each tuned so that its height-to-width ratio is one degree of the just major scale. The shelf is the octave: eight tones from C to C′, six and eight joined. Click a pot or press keys 1 to 8.

    Extrapolation 2 · the lathe

    Six against eight, spinning

    Revolve the profile and keep the polygons: the neck's cross-section stays a hexagon and the body's an octagon, blending through the shoulder. As it turns, every neck face that passes the front sounds a high note and every body face a low one. A 6 and 8 vessel plays a 3-against-4 polyrhythm, pitched at its own interval.

    Extrapolation 3 · the alphabet

    Your name in clay

    Each letter picks its own polygons, neck and swell, then gets tuned to a scale degree. A word becomes a row of pots and a short melody. Same letters, same pots, every time.

    Extrapolation 4 · the hunt

    The kiln keeps the rare ones

    The book's pot is special twice over: its ratio is a clean interval, and its perimeter is within one percent of the circle it came from. Most random pots manage one or neither. Throw at random; a pot within 6 cents of an interval and within 1% of π lands here.

    The system

    Five rules, one circle

    Everything comes from one radius

    No free numbers. Sizes, positions and curves are reached from the first circle by compass and straightedge, so a design can always be redrawn by hand.

    Black is given, red is derived

    Color carries meaning. Ink marks what you start with, graphite marks the working, and red marks what the construction produced.

    Keep the construction visible

    The working lines are ornament. Show them faint behind the result, and let replays reveal how a form was made.

    Every proportion is audible

    Any ratio in the system has a sound. Name ratios by interval, and test a layout by listening to it.

    Near is the point

    The pot's perimeter is 1.010π, not π. Keep the small error and display it. It shows where the drawing and the ideal disagree.

    Further

    Where else the recipe goes

    The engine underneath is small: two polygons, some deleted edges, a three-point arc, and a measurement. Change what the output is used for and you get very different things.

    Type

    A vessel typeface

    Use the deleted edges as the counters of letters. Each (neck, body) pair becomes a glyph skeleton, and the alphabet above becomes a real font.

    Space

    Rooms at 16 : 15

    Run the recipe at room scale: a hexagonal alcove over an octagonal hall. The room's proportion is a musical interval, and it would ring at that interval too.

    Sets

    Chords that nest

    Three vessels tuned 4 : 5 : 6 sized to fit inside one another, like nesting bowls. Stack them and you hold a major triad.

    Lineage

    Pots that breed

    Make the neck of one vessel the body of the next, each a fifth above its parent. You get a family tree that climbs the circle of fifths.

    Surface

    A floor of broken pots

    Cut each pot back into its polygons. Octagons tile with squares and hexagons tile alone, so the floor is a shelf taken apart.

    Making

    Templates for the wheel

    Export the profile as a potter's rib, a laser-cut stencil or a lathe path. Copy SVG above is the first step toward that.

    Interface

    Buttons that chime

    Cut avatars, buttons and progress rings from the same recipes. A progress bar fills a pot and sounds its interval when it is done.

    Listening

    Hum a pot

    Hum two notes, measure the interval, throw the vessel that matches. The ratio works in both directions: shape to sound and sound to shape.
