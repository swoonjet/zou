Compass Grammar

The pot in the book is one sentence. The words it's made of are a handful of compass and straightedge moves: draw a circle, overlap two, walk the radius, take a diagonal, cut from the corners, delete, join, measure. Break those out and you can write new sentences forever, and every one can still be drawn by hand.

Part 1 · the alphabet

Eight moves hidden in one drawing

Each move takes what is already on the page and gives back something new: a point, a length, a ratio. Black was given, grey is the working, red is what the move produced.

Part 2 · the machine

Random sentences, exact geometry

A seed picks a sequence of moves and which points they use. Symmetry repeats every move around the center. Then one rule does the deleting: keep only the region that sits inside exactly k circles. That single number replaces “parts of the octagon are deleted”, and changing it reveals completely different shapes inside the same construction.

      The system

      Rules for anything built this way

      Only constructible numbers

      No length is typed in. Every size is reached from the first radius by compass and straightedge, so 1, √2, √3, ½ and φ are allowed and 0.37 is not.

      Shapes are recipes

      Store a design as its list of moves, not its outline. The recipe can be redrawn, replayed, varied by one step, or handed to someone with a real compass.

      Delete by counting

      Choose regions by how many circles contain them. It keeps deletion lawful and makes every result a cousin of every other result from the same construction.

      Black given, grey working, red derived

      Color always says where a line came from. The working stays on the page, faint, as ornament and as proof.

      Every length can be heard

      Any length divided by the first radius is a frequency ratio. A construction can be played back as the sequence of circles it draws.

      Sound

      Constructions as scores

      Each move draws circles; each circle's radius is a pitch. A recipe is a melody in irrational intervals: √2 is a tritone, √3 sits near a harmonic seventh.

      measure

      Motion

      Walks that don't close

      Walk the compass with a chord that isn't the radius and the points never meet. Animate the chord length and the walk becomes a slow spiral that locks into polygons at constructible values.

      walk · measure

      Play

      Find the recipe

      Show only the red result and ask for the moves that make it, in as few as possible. Scoring by move count rewards seeing the hidden circles.

      all eight

      Surface

      Repeats that tile

      Take a 4- or 6-fold result and repeat it on a square or hexagonal grid. Overlapping neighbors create new regions you never drew, which you can delete by depth again.

      symmetry · delete

      Time

      One move a day

      A calendar or journal that adds a seeded move each day. By the end of a month it has drawn a construction no one designed, and each day's page shows its own depth.
