Five Faces

Five typefaces, each built on a grid from the earlier experiments, and none of them on a rectangle. The first four share one simple letter skeleton, so you can see what the grid alone does to it. The fifth is drawn from scratch out of vesica arcs.

Face 1 · from the walk

Orbit

A polar grid of rings and spokes. Letters stand on a circle, so every horizontal stroke becomes an arc on a ring and every vertical becomes a spoke. Words close into seals, coins and clock faces.

Face 2 · from the sunflower

Seedhead

Each letter is a sunflower head of seeds placed by the golden angle. The skeleton decides which seeds ripen. No two seeds share a row or column, so the letters have no pixel grid at all, only a spiral.

Face 3 · from the cells

Comb

A honeycomb, the shape the Voronoi cells settled into when relaxed. Letters are filled in cell by cell. Stems step in a gentle zigzag, and diagonals at 60° come out perfectly clean.

Face 4 · from the Penrose tiling

Pentad

Drawn on a lattice of the Penrose tiling's thick rhombus. Its 72° sides lean every stem 18°, and every 45° corner of the skeleton becomes a pentagon angle of 36° or 126°. An italic that comes from the grid, not a slant.

Face 5 · from the vesica

Mandorla

Every curve is an arc of a circle, and curves meet in points, never in smooth joins. The O is a true vesica, M and W are pairs of equilateral arches, and S is an ogee. The faint circles are the compass settings each letter needs.
