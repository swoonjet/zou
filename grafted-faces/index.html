Grafted Faces

Traditional capitals, with thick stems, thin hairlines and round bowls, where the compass grid touches only one part of each letter. In each face, a single detail is grafted on from a construction and the rest stays conventional. The red parts are the grafts.

Face 1 · Graft: serifs · from the vesica

Lancet

A bracketed roman where only the serifs are geometric. Each serif is half a vesica: an arc whose radius equals the serif's own width, so it rises exactly 13.4% of that width. Small arcs bracket it to the stem. Arms and round endings get the same half-vesica, standing upright.

Face 2 · Graft: terminals · from the golden spiral

Coil

A high-contrast Didone with hairline serifs. Only where a curve ends, on C, G, J and S, does it keep turning: a hairline spiral of quarter arcs shrinking by φ, ending in a ball. The squares show the whirl each curl is drawn in.

Face 3 · Graft: ink traps · from the sacred cut

Octavo

A heavy grotesque, plain everywhere except where strokes meet. Each join is cut by a small circle centered on the corner, the same move that found the octagon. At small sizes the cuts fill in with ink; large, they become the face's signature.

Face 4 · Graft: stroke endings · from one pair of circles per line

Horizon

A plain sans whose stroke ends are all cut by the same two concentric circles, one for the cap line and one for the baseline. Each line of text gets its own pair, so short words arch more and long lines barely curve. No letter is distorted, only trimmed.

Face 5 · Graft: crossbars and arms · from the vesica

Lens

A sans with ordinary stems and bowls, where every crossbar and free arm is a vesica lens: thickest in the middle, pointed where it meets the stem or ends in air. The H's bar, the arms of E and F, and the top of T all become leaves.
