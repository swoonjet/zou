Drawn Faces

Four faces where the construction is the letter. Every curve is one whole arc you could set on a compass, and where strokes meet, and at what angle, comes from the geometry, not from a grid of small pieces. Mandorla returns from the first set; three new faces join it.

Face 1 · From the vesica

Mandorla

Every curve is an arc of a circle, and curves meet in points, never in smooth joins. The O is a true vesica, M and W are pairs of equilateral arches, and S is an ogee.

Face 2 · From Hippocrates' lunes

Lune

Every stroke is a crescent: the sliver between two arcs that share their ends. Its thickness swells and tapers on its own, so the face has the thick-and-thin rhythm of a pen, without one. Heavy strokes run down-left to up-right like a broad nib; hairlines run the other way.

Face 3 · From the golden spiral

Whorl

Every curve is a quarter circle. Where a stroke ends, it keeps turning, each quarter shrinking by φ, and curls into a small golden spiral. The faint squares are the whirling squares each curl is drawn in.

Face 4 · From the sacred cut

Notch

The octagon was found by swinging arcs from the corners of a square. Here every corner of the letter gets the same move: a quarter arc centered on the corner itself, cutting it away. The result curves inward wherever other faces would round outward.
